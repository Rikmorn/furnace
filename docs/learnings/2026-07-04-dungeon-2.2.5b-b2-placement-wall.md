## TL;DR

- The generator (`topology.ts`: `_planTopology` + `generateWorldGraph`) works — produces valid `WorldGraph`s, deterministic, unit-tested over 20 seeds. **This is real, done, and green.**
- Feeding those graphs to `layoutWorld` (the 2.2.5a collision-aware placer) places **≈0%** of generated worlds. Target was ≥96% multi-seed, ≥80% on the reduced-config acceptance probe.
- Three root causes were peeled back in sequence, each revealing the next:
  1. **BLOCK 1 (FIXED, committed `ed0edba`):** `occupancy.ts` false-rejected every ROTATED voxel grid (`solid.yaw !== 0 → return true`). Fixed with an exact per-cell rotated test. Validated: a `mouths=1` cave off a stub anchor went 1/8 → 8/8.
  2. **BLOCK 2 (FIXED, committed `272375d`):** caves' whole-grid-AABB envelope is 93–96% AIR; Rule 1 (`checkPieceEnvelope`) treated it as a solid barrier, blocking dense packing. Fixed with honest compound `envelopes` (`RegionData.envelopes?: Aabb[]` = hub box + bore slabs + collar bounds). Property-tested (23k assertions). **But this did NOT meet its own acceptance (stub 0/10, buildWorld 0/3)** — unlike BLOCK 1.
  3. **RESIDUAL (NOT fixed; appears architectural):** the incremental placer orients each node to satisfy only its TREE-seating edge; it has no mechanism to also orient a node so its SECOND (loop/ring-closing) edge is realizable. Plus a portal-exemption sizing bug and a generator reserve-guard hole (both found, fixes known — see below). Best placement rate achieved after applying the two found fixes: **~7.5%** (prototyper's number, not independently reproduced — see Confidence).
- **Proven:** generator-side tuning does NOT fix it — a zero-cave control (all box rooms) reproduced the identical failure signatures. So the earlier "constrain the caves" direction is invalidated.

## The blocker — full evidence chain

**Symptom:** `layoutWorld(generateWorldGraph(anchor, seed), seed)` throws `layout: could not place node "sX-rY" within N attempts (<histogram>)` for ~all seeds/anchors/scales.

**Measured placement rates (stub anchor unless noted):**

| Config | Rate | Dominant rejection |
|---|---|---|
| `mouths=1` single cave (BLOCK-1 bisect) | 8/8 | — (this is the ONLY reliably-placeable case) |
| 2-room world | 5/10 | mixed |
| 4-room | 4/10 | clearance-solid |
| 8-room (acceptance config) | **0/10** | `clearance-solid:s0-r0 ×33` |
| TREE-like (sectors[2,2], loopChance 0) | 1/12 | `clearance-solid:s0-r0 ×7` |
| `buildWorld` off real authored level (world-b2-0..2) | 0/3 | `envelope-envelope:authored ×22-24` + `clearance-solid:s0-r0` |
| Best with prototyper's A2+B fixes applied | ~3/40 (7.5%) | `edge:facing` / `envelope-envelope` on interior nodes |

**Three theme-agnostic placer/generator issues (all verified):**

1. **Portal-exemption undersized** (`layout.ts portalExemption`). Its vertical reach was `headroom + ENCLOSURE_TOP_PAD` ≈ 3.35 m (headroom = connector door height 2.8 m), but a cave's own collar lintel reaches ~4.0 m and the hub ceiling ~7 m. So the entry connector's clearance clips the cave's own rock ABOVE the exemption → `clearance-solid:s0-r0`, at ALL yaw candidates (NOT a rotation effect — this is why "constrain cave mouths" doesn't help). **Prototyper's fix ("A2"):** raise the exemption ceiling to `max(headroom + ENCLOSURE_TOP_PAD, 10 m)`.
   - **A2 has a real side effect + companion cost:** changing which candidates the placer accepts flipped the hand-world `upperB` forced-stairs edge to a steeper run and broke `area-traversal.gpu` — because `world.ts`'s hand-picked `lengthRange:[8,13]` for that edge was ALREADY below `minWalkableRun(17,"stairs")≈14.2 m` and only "worked" by an unrelated false-reject. **A2 is not adoptable without auditing every hand-authored edge length in `world.ts` against `minWalkableRun`.** The prototyper fixed only the one edge the test suite caught (`upperB → [14.5,18]`).

2. **Joint multi-edge orientation (the architectural residual).** After A2, the dominant remaining rejection is `edge:facing`/`envelope-envelope` on interior nodes of degree ≥2. Mechanism (prototyper's, corroborated): the incremental placer seats a node by `join`-mating ONE portal to its already-placed parent (its tree edge), sampling length × yaw offsets. It has NO mechanism to also orient the node so its OTHER edges (loop-closing, ring-closing, or a second child) are realizable. The topology produces many such nodes by design (cycles-first ring + intra-sector loops + branching). So most generated graphs have edges the placer can't satisfy → backtrack → exhaust → fail. **This is not fixable by generator tuning** — proven by a zero-cave control (cave weight forced to 0, pure box rooms) reproducing the identical failure signatures on the same seeds.

3. **Generator reserve-guard hole** (`topology.ts _planTopology`). For some small configs (e.g. `{targetRooms:8, sectors:[3,3]}`, ~6.6% of seeds) it throws `no free portal pair to close the macro ring — reserve guard failed`. Task 4's 20 default-config seeds never exercised this. **Prototyper's fix ("B"):** floor the last-room capacity sample to cover the sector's still-owed ring/macro duty (`roomsLeft === 0` case). Verified: 0/2000 throws vs 131/2000 baseline. This one is clean and standalone.

## Confidence assessment (my diagnosis was overturned TWICE — read this critically)

- I first diagnosed the 0% as a cave mouth-DIRECTION problem (connectors clipping opposing mouths). **Overturned by the orchestrator (BLOCK 1):** the real bug was the occupancy `yaw !== 0` shortcut. My mistake: I saw the histogram `clearance-solid`/`envelope-envelope` and theorized about cave geometry instead of checking whether those were EXACT hits or the code's `// conservative` over-rejects.
- I then framed the BLOCK-2 residual as purely "envelope over-claim." **Partially right** (envelope over-claim was real, BLOCK 2 fixed it) but insufficient — the portal-exemption bug + joint-orientation limit remained.
- The "7.5% ceiling" is the PROTOTYPER's number, measured in its worktree WITH its A2+B fixes. **I did NOT independently reproduce 7.5%.** My independent numbers are PRE-A2 (so `clearance-solid` dominates in mine): ~0–8% across configs. The qualitative conclusion (far below 80%; residual is theme-agnostic and survives the two found fixes) is robust across both measurements; the exact 7.5% is not independently verified.
- The "architectural — needs a placer redesign" conclusion rests on: (a) the zero-cave control (strong), (b) the mechanism reasoning about join orienting only one edge (plausible, code-consistent), (c) two failed search-widening probes (±90° yaw, 100k attempts) that didn't help. It is well-evidenced but was produced under the same investigative pressure that overturned my two earlier calls — **a fresh session should re-verify the joint-orientation mechanism directly** (instrument `layout.ts candidatesFor`/the accept loop for a degree-≥2 interior node and confirm no candidate satisfies both its edges) before committing to a full redesign.

## Decision space for re-planning

1. **Placer redesign (unblocks the vision, biggest effort).** Replace `layout.ts`'s one-edge-at-a-time incremental search with a placer that jointly satisfies each node's MULTIPLE edges — e.g. orient toward the most-constrained edge with a lookahead, or a proper constraint-satisfaction / backtracking search over (position × yaw) that checks all incident already-placed edges. This is a new sub-epic, not a Task-6 tweak. Land B (clean) + A2 (with the `world.ts` edge audit) first as genuine bug fixes.

2. **Simplify the placement target (medium effort, less interconnection).** Constrain the GENERATOR to near-trees (drop most loop/ring-closing edges, cap node degree so few nodes have a second edge), land B + A2, and prototype whether the current placer then hits the bar. Trades the "interconnected dungeon" character. NOTE: my tree-like test placed only 1/12 PRE-A2 — this path's viability is UNVERIFIED post-A2 and must be prototyped before betting on it.

3. **Descope generated-world placement for this slice (consolidation).** Keep the generator (Tasks 1–6b) as a validated library. Land B (and optionally A2 + the audit) as clean bug-fix commits. Do NOT flip `main.ts` — keep the hand-authored world. Re-plan generated placement as a future effort. Lowest risk; preserves all the real work; ships nothing broken.

**My recommendation:** #3 (consolidate + re-plan) or a re-plan that starts with #1 as its own scoped effort — because the point-fix chain is provably not converging (0% → still ~7.5% after two fixes) and the remaining gap is architectural, not a tuning problem.

## Orchestrator verification addendum (2026-07-04, post-handback)

An independent control the executor couldn't run was run at the freeze point (branch @ `272375d`,
pre-A2/B): generated 12-room graphs with ALL cycle-closing edges surgically stripped post-hoc
(union-find; pure probe, no source change) vs the same graphs intact, against the REAL authored
anchor. Result: **FULL 0/12, TREE-ONLY 0/12 — identical entry-cave histograms**
(`envelope-envelope:authored ×22` + `edge:clearance-solid:s0-r0 ×10`, ≈32/35 candidates dead at
`s0-r0`). Interpretation: the residual is **two layers**, and the joint-orientation limit is NOT
the binding constraint at this commit —

- **Layer 1 (binds first, theme-agnostic):** entry-piece seating. Yaw-offset candidates die on
  envelope-vs-authored (conservative rotated-AABB inflation of 15–25 m pieces at 6–12 m entry
  lengths); straight candidates die on own-rock clearance above the undersized portal exemption
  (= A2). This is what the zero-cave control was actually reproducing.
- **Layer 2 (binds after A2/B, the prototype's ~7.5% regime):** multi-edge interior nodes — the
  joint-orientation mechanism described above. Plausible and prototype-corroborated, but per the
  Confidence section it still needs direct instrumentation before "full placer redesign" is
  accepted as the only shape.

Two concrete NON-redesign candidates for Layer 2, untested by the (blind) search-widening probes,
recorded as B2-continuation design inputs:
1. **Portal-assignment freedom** — generated doors/mouths on a piece are identical specs, so the
   placer may legally choose WHICH free portal serves a loop/ring edge on BOTH endpoints (the
   generator's slot assignment is an arbitrary labelling, not geometry). This multiplies loop-
   closure odds without new search architecture.
2. **Aimed orientation** — when the node being placed has an edge to an already-placed partner,
   the piece yaw that points a chosen free portal at that partner is computable in closed form at
   candidate time; today candidates are enumerated blind and loop edges checked post-hoc. Combined
   with (1), the candidate loop becomes (door-assignment × length × aimed-yaw±wiggle), still inside
   the existing Edgar-shaped skeleton + backtracking.

Also relevant prior-art pointer for the continuation: the 2026-07-01 research doc records that our
placer deliberately RELAXED Edgar's cycles-first ordering ("closing a cycle edge degrades to a
check") — Edgar lays out cycles first as chains from precomputed configuration spaces precisely
because tree-first placement leaves cycle closure to luck. The continuation's research pass should
weigh: cycles-first chain decomposition vs tree-first + (1)+(2) vs full CSP.
