**Epistemic key:** `[V]` verified from a cited source · `[K]` established general knowledge ·
`[I]` inference about fit to our pipeline.

Grounded in the dungeon's actual pipeline (verified this session): heterogeneous "regions" —
SDF/density-field **caves** (Surface-Nets meshed, collided via field-derived voxel proxies) and
box-architecture **rooms** (cuboid walls/pillars/dais+steps). Composed today by: a region
declares `Connection` portals (position + OUTWARD facing normal + width/height + kind
`door|tunnel-mouth`); `compose.ts placeRoom` **cardinal-snaps** a room door to a cave mouth
(`snapCardinal` + `yawMap`, only 0/90/180/270°) and bridges with a flat-floored vestibule; the
authored `LEVEL_BOXES` spine ↔ generated wing seam is **hand-tuned** (`main.ts AREA_ORIGIN`
`GATE-TUNE` + overlap coverage). Limitations to break: ~2D join (cardinal yaw + coplanar flat
seams only); only cave-mouth→room-door really supported; one height level.

## 1. Modular/portal connection — the standard data model + the join

The shipped pattern is **named typed sockets ("snap points")**. Unreal's Modular Snap System
matches a source socket to a target socket on **socket name (type), distance, and the angle
between the sockets' forward vectors**, then rigidly moves/rotates the source so both sockets
share one world location + orientation [V: Inu Games MSS docs; StraySpark UE5 comparison]. The
academic equivalent, **Snappable Meshes**, gives each piece "connectors" with a colour
(compatibility tag) + pins (alignment + footprint) and joins only where colours match
[V: arXiv 2108.00056]. **Our `Connection` is already this model** — `kind` = the colour/name,
`width/height` = the pin footprint, the normal = the forward vector. [I]

**The join transform** seats exit-portal frame B onto entrance-portal frame A:
`T_A · R_A · R_180 · R_B⁻¹ · T_B⁻¹` — translate so B's origin lands on A's origin, rotate so B's
outward normal becomes A's *inward* normal (the 180° flip), leaving a yaw + height offset
[V: gamedev.net portal-portal rotation].

**Verdict — "yaw + height, not full 3D": RIGHT, matches shipped practice.** Walkable
first-person worlds keep a global gravity-up; production modular systems constrain rotation to
yaw and let height float free (DunGen places doorways at differing heights but never
rolls/pitches a room [V: DunGen multiple-floors]). Full 6-DOF portal matching is for
zero-G/Prey-style/non-Euclidean spaces. Keep yaw + height — that's the norm, not a shortcut.
**This general transform SUBSUMES our cardinal-snap** (90° becomes the case where the angle
lands on an axis), so `snapCardinal`/`yawMap` stop being a special path. [I]

## 2. Connector insertion to bridge a gap

Two families. **(a) Routed/carved** — a pathfinder *is* the connector. VAZGRIZ's 3D dungeon
runs a modified **A\* through a voxel grid** between two room portals; to change height it lays a
staircase as a multi-cell move at a fixed **rise:run of 1:2**, reserves **two cells of headroom
above the stair**, and rejects self-intersecting paths — the stair is auto-sized to the exact
height delta the route hits [V: VAZGRIZ, Procedurally Generated Dungeons]. **(b) Spline/parametric**
— Unreal PCG draws a spline between anchors and sweeps a corridor profile along it, deforming to
the gap [V: Epic, Procedural Room Generation with Splines + PCG]. TinyKeep's 2D version is the
flat degenerate case: straight or **L-shaped** corridors between door openings [V: TinyKeep,
GameDev.net].

**Verdict — "choose connector type by the gap": directionally right, mildly naive in the
dispatch.** A type-by-threshold table (small Δh→ramp, big Δh→stairs/shaft, pure horizontal→
corridor) is a fine first cut, but the robust approach is a **router with hard constraints**
(max walkable slope, stair rise:run, min headroom, min run length) that **fails and re-routes**
rather than emitting a too-steep ramp. **Risk: a naive threshold can emit a 50° ramp our
`CharacterMover.isWalkable` rejects.** → **Bake the same slope/step constants the controller
uses (`SLOPE_LIMIT_COS` cos55°, `STEP_HEIGHT` 0.4) into the connector chooser so a chosen
connector is walkable BY CONSTRUCTION.** [I]

## 3. Verticality without feeling mechanical

The well-regarded split is exactly the proposed one. **Continuous-field games** (No Man's Sky,
Deep Rock Galactic, Astroneer) get organic slopes/shafts/overhangs *for free* because geometry
is an isosurface of one density field — NMS generates a voxel field then polygonizes it
[V: GDC, Continuous World Generation in No Man's Sky]; DRG carves its whole level into one
marching-cubes field so tunnels + chambers flow into each other [V/second-hand: DRG marching-cubes
overview — primary Steam dev blog unreachable, treat DRG specifics as second-hand]. **Built
spaces** instead get explicit generated stairs (VAZGRIZ rooms are multi-floor; the
tetrahedralization guarantees cross-floor edges that A\* resolves into staircases [V]).

**Verdict — "field-slopes for caves + architectural stairs for rooms + context-chosen
connectors": a sound, industry-matching reconciliation, NOT a compromise.** No widely-used
engine unifies the two into one representation; they keep field caves + built rooms as distinct
realizations and reconcile **at the connector**. **The key correction to our "north star":** the
rejected thing was a **stacked-floor *feel*** (discrete platters + teleport stairs), not a
*graph*. Topology can be a graph (§5) while *realization* stays continuous/contextual — the
graph decides what-connects-to-what; the field/stair generators decide how it *looks*.
**Graph ≠ stacked-floor feel.** (And continuous slopes/shafts are explicitly IN the north star.)

## 4. Clean seams at height

Dominant mitigation: **don't have two surfaces meet — make one continuous surface span the
seam.** DRG/NMS avoid seams by meshing *one* field [V]. When two independently-built pieces must
meet (our voxel-cave ↔ cuboid-room case), the shipped fixes: **(1) a connector piece that
physically overlaps both sides** (our flat vestibule is exactly this — the right instinct), **(2)
snap both portal floor planes to a common height before joining** (height offset applied to the
whole piece, not the floor edge), **(3) deliberate overlap coverage on collision** rather than
precise abutment (the lesson our own 2.2.1 work encoded — widen the grid to overlap; off-grid
neighbours treated as solid). For multi-height: the connector's floor must be the **load-bearing
surface across the Δh transition** (a ramp/stair *slab*, not two floor edges hoping to touch),
and its collider should overlap both endpoints by ≥1 cell so a fast player can't tunnel the
seam. [V/I]

## 5. Generation graph for a large multi-segment world (feeds 2.2.5)

Scalable standard: **TinyKeep** — scatter pieces → triangulate centres → MST for guaranteed
connectivity → add back ~15% of edges for loops [V: TinyKeep, GameDev.net]. The clean **3D**
extension to copy is **VAZGRIZ**: 3D Delaunay **tetrahedralization** (Bowyer-Watson) → Prim's MST
→ re-add edges; because tetrahedra span vertices on different floors, the connection graph
naturally contains **multi-height edges**, which the stair router realizes [V: VAZGRIZ]. For more
*authored* control over loops/branches/dead-ends, **graph-grammar / cyclic generation**
(Unexplored grows the layout from rewrite rules so loops are intentional) [V: Boris the Brave,
Dungeon Generation in Unexplored; Liapis PCG-Book dungeons chapter].

**Recommendation: 3D graph (tetrahedralization + MST + ~15% loop edges) for topology + our
portal/connector system for realization.** Scales to many segments at multiple heights *because*
height variation lives in the graph edges, and each edge becomes a §2 connector chosen by its
actual Δposition/Δheight/Δyaw. We already have the small-scale TinyKeep idea; the upgrades are
(a) take the triangulation to 3D so edges cross heights, (b) replace cardinal-snap with the §1
portal transform. Theme the topology (natural cave-network vs artificial dungeon-grid). [I]

## Net assessment of the stated direction

- **"yaw + height, not full 3D"** — Right. Matches shipped FP/gravity practice; 6-DOF is
  non-Euclidean/zero-G only.
- **"auto-insert a connector sized to the gap"** — Right in spirit; make it a *constraint-checked
  router* sharing the controller's walkability constants, not a bare threshold table — else
  unwalkable ramps.
- **"field-slopes for caves + stairs for rooms"** — Right, and explicitly the industry split
  (NMS/DRG fields vs VAZGRIZ/DunGen stairs). Reconcile at the connector; make the connector the
  seam-spanning surface.
- **The one revision** — a topology *graph* is NOT the "mechanical" thing the north star
  rejected (that was discrete-platter *realization*). Adopt the 3D-tetrahedralized graph for
  topology with confidence; keep realization continuous/contextual.

## Decisions this fed (Slice 2.2.4 / 2.2.5)

- Remaining Epic 2 arc re-scoped to **Composability & Authorability**: connect + lay out a world
  from EXISTING content kinds; deep content/field generation (rich biomes, implicit-infinite
  streaming, LLM-authored intent) → **Epic 3**.
- **2.2.4 = Seams (the connection primitive):** general portal join (yaw+height, subsumes
  cardinal-snap) + connector insertion (corridor/ramp/stair-run/shaft) as a constraint-checked
  router sharing `CharacterMover` walkability constants + clean multi-height seam (connector floor
  spans the join, overlap-covered). **Scope = option A:** the primitive + a small hand-composed
  multi-level arrangement proven on the EXISTING dungeon pieces (give them portals). Verticality =
  context-driven (field slopes/shafts for caves; architectural stairs for rooms). Cardinal-snap +
  the hand-tuned authored↔generated seam are TARGETED for retirement here but FULLY retired in
  2.2.5.
- **2.2.5 = Layout (the world graph):** themed (natural/artificial), cyclic-capable (mazes/loops),
  3D tetrahedralization → MST → loop edges, weaving authored landmarks into generated regions;
  replaces the hand-authored spine → full cardinal-snap + special-seam retirement.
- **2.3 = Editor cockpit:** now has a real world-model (graph + portals) to generate→curate→freeze.
