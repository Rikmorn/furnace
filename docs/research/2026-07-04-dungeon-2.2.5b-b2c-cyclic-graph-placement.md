# Placing generated cyclic world graphs — research for the B2 continuation (B2c)

> **Status:** Pre-decision research feeding the 2.2.5b-B2 continuation ("B2c" — generated-graph
> placement), produced 2026-07-04 by a 4-lane cited prior-art pass (academic cycles-first school ·
> shipped modular-kit placers · cyclic-generation/grid-embedding school · solver school) plus an
> empirical counterfactual probe run in the B2 prototype worktree. Context: the B2 topology
> generator landed green but `layoutWorld` places ~0–7.5% of generated seeds vs an ≥80% bar —
> evidence chain in `docs/learnings/2026-07-04-dungeon-2.2.5b-b2-placement-wall.md` (Layer 1 =
> entry-seating bugs with known fixes; Layer 2 = multi-edge joint orientation). This pass answers
> the question that doc's addendum posed: **cycles-first chain decomposition vs tree-first +
> portal-assignment freedom + aimed orientation vs full CSP** — so the redesign-vs-extend decision
> is made on priors, not vibes. It extends (and does not re-cover)
> `docs/research/2026-07-01-dungeon-2.2.5-layout-placement-and-megastructure.md`, which already
> recorded that 2.2.5a deliberately relaxed Edgar's cycles-first ordering ("closing a cycle edge
> degrades to a check").

## 1. The academic school (Ma et al. 2014 / Edgar) — our two failure layers are their two ablations

- **[V]** Ma, Vining, Lefebvre, Sheffer 2014 (Eurographics) decomposes the input planar graph
  into **chains** via a planar embedding used only for ordering: faces → cycles, smallest cycle
  first, neighbouring faces next, acyclic components last (BFS). Rationale, verbatim: "cyclical
  chains are significantly more constrained than open-ended ones, as in the former case the
  blocks must form a cycle, with the last and first ones touching one another." A **whole chain
  is the unit of placement and backtracking** — an entire cycle is laid out jointly into the
  partial layout (greedy config-space init + simulated-annealing repair, ~15 stored partial
  layouts for stack-based backtracking across chains). Cycle closure is a hard constraint
  *inside* the chain solve, never a post-hoc check.
- **[V]** **Configuration spaces** are the candidate generator: for a block pair, the analytic
  locus of positions where the moving block touches-but-doesn't-overlap the fixed one (line
  segments, precomputed per shape pair; door positions folded in). For a node with **2+ placed
  neighbours, candidates come from the INTERSECTION of the pairwise configuration spaces** — and
  when the intersection is empty, from the **maximal non-empty intersection** ("we locally
  maximize the number of contacts satisfied"). That sentence is the paper's answer to our
  Layer 2.
- **[V]** The ablation table maps onto our failure layers exactly. Without configuration spaces
  (≈ our blind yaw/length sampling): first-layout success 0.4–0.7, **ten-layout success 0 even on
  the simplest graphs** ("with random position changes the method was never able to satisfy
  contacts exactly"). Without incremental chain structure (≈ our loop-edges-as-checks): 0.7/0.76
  first, 0 tenth. Both ingredients necessary; exact candidates are the bigger lever. The paper's
  §2 explicitly classes greedy open-position-queue placers as handling "only acyclic layouts."
- **[V]** Success/perf: ≥90–100% per-seed success on 40-node cyclic graphs (the one sub-perfect
  input has multiple *interconnected* cycles); Edgar's tile-based reimplementation solves a
  41-node cyclic graph in 0.62 s avg. Edgar's own guidance: recommend **< 30 rooms** (< 20 for
  comfort), "start with 0–1 cycles," "interconnected cycles are even harder… make sure that the
  cycles do not have any rooms in common."
- **[V]** **Fixed orientation throughout.** Ma-2014 blocks are fixed during optimization
  (rotation only as pre-enriched discrete variants); Edgar allows 90°-multiples only, on a grid.
  **[V]** Verticality: Ma-2014 multi-floor = stacked 2D solves with matching stairwell
  constraints; Edgar-Unity 3D "works completely in 2D," forbids room-over-room, and its **"Fix
  Elevations Inside Cycles" feature bans elevation-changing templates inside cycles outright** —
  no system in this school closes a vertical cycle geometrically. **[I]** Our generator already
  compensates on the generator side (B2's plan pass assigns elevations during growth so ring/loop
  edges close in height by construction) — that design choice is exactly what this school's
  limitation demands.
- **[I]** Fit: our pieces never touch — they mate portal-to-portal through a straight connector —
  so once a portal assignment is chosen, **piece yaw is pinned in closed form** (portal alignment)
  and run length is the main scalar DOF. The transferable idea is **exact-locus candidate
  enumeration** (the 1-neighbour case degenerates to "aimed orientation"; the 2-neighbour case is
  a small analytic intersection solve over position × yaw × two run lengths), not the
  segment-table representation. We also don't need the planar-embedding step — the B2 generator
  *constructs* its ring and loops, so cycles are known by construction.
- **[V]** No documented hybrid of "chain-decomposition ordering + purely greedy first-accept
  placement with no stochastic repair" was found; Ma's per-chain init is greedy config-space
  placement with SA as the repair layer, and Edgar truncated SA hard (m=100, strict early-abort)
  rather than removing it. **[I]** Adopting cycles-first ordering while keeping node-at-a-time
  first-accept has no precedent and forfeits the mechanism that makes the ordering work.

## 2. Shipped modular-kit placers — aimed mating is unanimous; nobody ships demanded free-space cycles

- **[V]** **Exact portal-to-portal mating is the industry-standard candidate generation; blind
  yaw sampling has no shipped precedent found.** Warframe (Brewer, Game AI Pro 2021, full placer
  pseudo-code): "Level blocks can be rotated and transformed as necessary to line up compatible
  portals"; a candidate = (frontier portal × compatible portal on the new block × block pool),
  one deterministic transform, first no-overlap fit accepted, DFS with single-step unwind.
  DunGen rotates a tile 90/180/270° "to match the orientation of an adjacent Tile's doorway";
  Dungeon Architect Snap stitches exact connection points. Orientation is an *output of the
  mating equation*, never a sampled dimension.
- **[V]** **Portal-assignment freedom is likewise standard.** Warframe iterates every open portal
  on every placed block × every portal on the candidate block; DunGen defaults to entering/exiting
  "through any available doorway on the tile," with sockets/Entrance/Exit as opt-in *restrictions*
  on that freedom. Portal identity is a compatibility vocabulary, not a fixed graph-slot label.
- **[V]** **No shipped free-space door-mating system closes graph-DEMANDED cycles.** Warframe
  layouts are trees (a linear block-type template + branches; unused portals get **caps**; blocks
  with internal "loopbacks" are flagged as layout hazards). DunGen FAQ, verbatim: "Loops (paths
  that connect back up to the rest of the dungeon) are **not** supported"; its
  Doorway-Connection-Chance feature only connects doorways that "coincidentally overlap" and is
  vendor-labeled "unreliable." Dungeon Architect's free-space Snap builder: "Cycles are not
  supported"; its Snap **Grid Flow** builder gets cyclic paths only by fixing module sizes into a
  3D lattice.
- **[V]** **Failure economics:** the standard shape is a backtracking inner loop + a
  throw-away-and-retry outer loop (DunGen ships retry-forever in builds; bounded only in-editor —
  the 2.2.5a research already flagged our bounded version as the deliberate inversion). Warframe
  meets its bar mostly **offline, by co-designing the content kit with the placer**: automated
  thousands-of-layouts tests per template, designers reshape portals/blocks until failures and
  excessive rollbacks vanish, pool sized so a layout consumes only ~15–20% of it, 1–2 portal
  types per template, dedicated **spacer blocks** ("portals opposite each other… to create
  distance between blocks to avoid overlaps").
- **[I]** Fit: our two extension candidates are not enhancements to industry practice — they ARE
  industry practice; the yaw-offset grid is the anomaly. And Warframe's kit-tuning lever maps to
  our generator params (door counts, room dims, connector length ranges are all ours to tune,
  with a seed-scan harness as the "automated thousands of layouts" analogue).

## 3. The cyclic-generation / grid-embedding school — closure slack is always bendability or adjacency

- **[V]** **Unexplored's celebrated cycles are not a placer achievement: the graph is born
  embedded.** Generation starts on a ~5×5 grid of graph nodes; the cycle is *drawn* as a ring of
  adjacent cells from step one; all cyclic-grammar rewriting only ANNOTATES nodes ("grid nodes
  are never deleted or moved"); the grid is then resolution-doubled for corridor cells and ×5'd
  into the tilemap. Cycle edges only ever exist between grid-adjacent cells → every cycle is a
  closed cell-ring **by construction**; there is no placement-failure mode to backtrack.
  **[I]** Importing the cyclic grammar without the grid substrate imports none of the guarantee.
- **[V]** **Enter the Gungeon** (authored loop "flows"): rooms added door-to-door on a tile grid;
  loop composites grow alternately from both ends of a line, preferring exit pairs that "bring
  the two unclosed ends of the loop closer together"; final closure picks an exit pair and builds
  a small rectangular room between them — "**Otherwise it pathfinds between the exits**, and
  makes a 'room' that is just a narrow corridor." **[I]** Even with authored loops + heuristic
  convergence + grid tiles, Gungeon keeps a *pathfound, bendable corridor as the escape hatch of
  last resort* — the loop closes because the final edge is the one edge allowed to be arbitrarily
  shaped.
- **[V]** Same pattern everywhere: TinyKeep re-adds ~8–15% of Delaunay edges as loops **after**
  rooms are positioned — viable only because every edge is realized as a straight-or-**L**
  corridor that can absorb any relative placement; Brogue finds loop doors opportunistically
  where geometry already touches (grid scan + A*-distance test); Cogmind's tunnelers make every
  edge a bendable dig; Deep Rock Galactic carves "narrow, winding tunnels" through voxel rock
  ("pearls on a string" — a tree anyway); Diablo 1 draws line corridors on a 40×40 grid and
  scraps-and-retries whole levels.
- **[V]** **"Straight rigid connectors + continuous yaw + demanded cycles" appears to be
  unprecedented.** The nearest survivor (Edgar, incl. its 3D mode) quantizes to a grid with
  90°-only rotation, places cycles FIRST from configuration spaces — and still warns users off
  cycle-heavy graphs. The 3D free-space systems (Warframe, DRG) are trees.
- **[I]** Fit: the closure slack that preserves our continuous-yaw, organic-piece identity is
  **connector bendability** — an L/dogleg (2-segment) routed connector turns a loop-closing edge
  from a rigid simultaneous constraint over all node poses (Σ edge-vectors = 0 with 2-DOF-rigid
  edges — exactly the measured Layer-2 failure) into a routable object, Gungeon-style "the last
  edge bends." Grid quantization would also work (Edgar-3D proves it) but costs the off-axis
  joins and continuous yaw the project deliberately built (2.2.4/2.2.5a).

## 4. The solver school — finite-domain walls; our placer is already a CSP search missing four techniques

- **[V]** ASP/SAT are **finite-domain**: "floating-point is permanently off-limits" (Smith &
  Mateas); no published ASP system embeds a room graph at continuous poses — spatial ASP is
  grid placements (6×6 mazes in 250 ms; a 10×10 board embedding; optimization blows up at 21×21),
  and the one ASP dungeon-graph paper (FDG 2018) is **tree-only and geometry-free**. Horswill's
  field report: stable-model debugging is brutal; for non-reachability PCG "a more conventional
  SAT or SMT solver would be more appropriate." MILP floor-plan work (MIQP/Gurobi 2018, GPLAN)
  is axis-aligned rectangles, wall-adjacency, no continuous rotation, no free-space connectors —
  a different problem. WFC (even DeBroglie's global Connected/**Loop**/Acyclic constraints) ranges
  over tile adjacency, not an input room graph — a detail layer, not a graph embedder.
- **[V]** **Solver-in-browser weight**, for the record: clingo-wasm is real and light
  (`clingo.wasm` ≈ 2.4 MB, deterministic by default — proportionate if we ever delegate a
  *discrete* subproblem like portal assignment or lock/key logic); z3-wasm is ~32 MB +
  SharedArrayBuffer/COOP-COEP; MILP has no browser path.
- **[V]** Our `layout.ts` is already a backtracking CSP search (static most-constrained-first
  order + randomized value sampling + bounded backtracking). The missing techniques, in CSP
  vocabulary, with documented payoffs: **exact candidate generation** (= configuration
  spaces/aimed mating); **forward checking** (validate ALL incident placed edges at candidate
  time, not post-hoc — AIMA: "an obvious partner" of MRV; subsumes backjumping); **cycles-first
  ordering** (= cycle cutset conditioning: instantiate the ring, the remainder is a tree);
  **seeded geometric restarts** (Refraction: up to four orders of magnitude on a hand-rolled DFS,
  the same shape as ours); **dynamic MRV** (recompute the order as placement proceeds — ours is
  computed once up front); optionally conflict-directed backjumping via the candidate-failure
  histogram we already collect.
- **[I]** Verdict: a full external solver is disproportionate — the two defining features of our
  problem (continuous pose, black-box occupancy predicate) are precisely what declarative solvers
  cannot ingest without first building the discretized candidate/collision tables that make the
  hand-rolled search work anyway. At 8–40 nodes the documented consensus is the Ma/Edgar shape:
  decomposition-aware ordering + exact candidates + backtracking + restarts.

## 5. Empirical probe — the counterfactual, measured in the prototype worktree

*(Probe: the preserved B2 prototype worktree — branch `272375d` + the prototype A2
portal-exemption, B reserve-guard, and `authored→upperB` length-retune edits — via an
instrumented copy of `layout.ts` with **verified 40/40 parity** against the real `layoutWorld`.
Tracks: "stub" = 8-room config off a stub slab anchor, seeds `b2d-0..39`; "authored" = DEFAULT
~30-room config off the real authored level, seeds `world-b2-0..9`. Independent of the
executor's numbers.)*

- **[M]** **The ~7.5% figure is confirmed exactly** (3/40 stub; 0/10 authored, in every mode).
- **[M]** **Failure taxonomy (37 thrown nodes): the post-A2/B residual is two-headed, so the
  "joint orientation is THE architectural limit" claim is only PARTLY confirmed.** 21/37
  failures have an active loop edge (dominant rejection `edge[loop]:facing` — directly
  instrumented: e.g. all 35 base candidates of one node fail the loop-facing check because yaw
  is slaved to the seat ray). But 16/37 failures have **no active loop edge at all** — they are
  degree-≥2 nodes dying on **fan-position packing** (sibling envelope-envelope + seat-corridor
  clearance-solid). Entry-cave throws are backtrack scapegoats for sibling packing behind them.
  Structural note: seat-edge candidates pass the facing gate by construction, so every
  `edge:facing` count in a histogram is a loop edge.
- **[M]** **The two non-redesign extensions are real but insufficient.** Frozen per-failure
  census (full extension space: closed-form aimed yaw ±wiggle × all spec-identical portal
  permutations on both endpoints × length grid): 37/40 failing contexts have ZERO acceptable
  candidates even with both extensions. End-to-end (extensions spliced into the real
  backtracking search, 4× attempt budget): base 7.5% → aimed-alone 7.5% (rescues zero) →
  **portal-freedom-alone 25%** → both 22.5% → best-of-portfolio 30%. A 4× budget alone rescues
  zero. Authored track: 0/10 in every mode. **Portal-assignment freedom is the load-bearing
  extension; aimed orientation pays only in combination** (it converts near-misses and cuts
  rescue attempt counts ~10×, but never rescues alone).
- **[M]** **Diagnosis of the unrescued 76%: position-space poverty.** Every candidate position
  is confined to a 35-point fan on the seat parent's ray (5 lengths × 7 yaw offsets). The
  unrescued failures are: sibling envelopes saturating that fan, connector clearance clipping
  walls at every fan point, and placed partners whose portal cones simply never intersect the
  fan (positional, not orientational). No orientation/relabeling trick reaches these — **the
  candidate POSITION space must be freed** (continuous/exact loci, parent-portal choice,
  chain-joint placement, or bendable connectors). Note the probe did NOT test position
  enrichment — its headroom is unmeasured; the 25–30% ceiling applies to orientation/relabeling
  extensions with positions still on the fan grid.
- **[M]** **Integration warning:** naively APPENDING extension candidates to the existing
  candidate list perturbs the backtracking search path and can LOSE baseline successes (2 base
  seeds regressed under "both"). Candidate-generation changes must be integrated into the
  deterministic ordering, not bolted on.
- Caveats: the frozen census is per-greedy-context (not proof of global infeasibility — the
  end-to-end matrix is the global measurement); aim targeted only the first active loop edge; a
  few timeout-marked runs ran under CPU contention (only one seed's rescue could conceivably
  flip with more budget).

## Net assessment — the B2c design space

- **Extend the skeleton, but the extension list is longer than the two candidates the wall doc
  named — and the probe measured those two as necessary-but-insufficient.** The four lanes
  converge on the standard toolbox (aimed mating transforms + portal-assignment freedom are
  unanimous shipped practice, the load-bearing ablation in the academic school, and the "exact
  candidate generation" CSP technique; forward checking + seeded restarts + dynamic ordering
  complete it) — but the probe measured orientation/relabeling extensions plateauing at 25–30%
  vs the ≥80% bar, with positions still confined to the seat-ray fan. **The measured binding
  constraint is candidate POSITION poverty**, whose fixes — continuous/exact position loci over
  (length × yaw-offset) regions (the configuration-space idea adapted to connector mating),
  parent-portal choice spreading siblings across portals, chain-joint placement — are exactly
  what the research lanes prescribe and the probe left unmeasured (its headroom is the open
  variable B2c bets on). Candidate changes must be *integrated* into the deterministic search
  ordering, not appended (measured regression risk).
- **Demanded cycles in free space need a closure mechanism nobody gets for free.** Three exist,
  composable: (a) **cycles-first chains** — lay each cycle out as the placement/backtracking
  unit with closure as an in-chain hard constraint (Edgar-faithful; heaviest; the only one with
  ≥90% measured success on 40-node cyclic graphs, but measured in fixed-orientation 2D);
  (b) **bendable loop connectors** — an L/dogleg route for loop/ring-closing edges only
  (Gungeon/TinyKeep slack; keeps straight connectors everywhere else; requires
  route/enclosure/clearance support for a 2-segment connector); (c) **generator-side softening**
  — emit loop edges as *preferences* the placer may satisfy opportunistically
  (DunGen-connection-chance school; cheapest, weakest guarantees — trades away the
  "interconnected dungeon by construction" character). The probe sharpens the choice: many loop
  failures are POSITIONAL (the partner's portal cone never intersects the seat fan), which
  chains solve by searching cycle-member positions jointly and bends solve by absorbing the
  residual offset — pure orientation fixes solve neither. Generator-side **cycle hygiene** is
  cheap insurance either way (Edgar: interconnected cycles are the hard case — keep cycles
  room-disjoint by construction).
- **The content kit is a success-rate lever, not just the search** (Warframe): door counts, room
  dims, connector length ranges, spacer-ish vestibule pieces, and a seed-scan harness as the
  automated thousands-of-layouts test. **[I]** Our generator already owns all of these knobs.
- **Verticality inside cycles is beyond every surveyed system** (Edgar-3D bans it outright).
  The B2 generator's close-elevation-during-growth design is the right compensation and should
  be kept load-bearing (flat or near-flat loop-closing edges by construction).
- **Ruled out on evidence:** grid quantization of the world (defeats the continuous-yaw/off-axis
  identity built in 2.2.4/2.2.5a); full external solvers (finite-domain wall, black-box
  predicates, weight); force-directed relaxation (already overturned 2026-07-01); adopting
  cycles-first *ordering* without chain-unit placement (no precedent, forfeits the mechanism).
